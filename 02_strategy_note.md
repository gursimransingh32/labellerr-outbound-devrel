# 2. Outreach strategy note

Labellerr is a small team (about 13 people, per Tracxn), so the goal is not volume. It is a high reply rate on about 20 well-researched accounts. Every first touch leads with a company-specific signal and offers a labeled sample instead of a deck. At robotics companies under about 300 people, the data-vendor decision sits with the CTO or head of robot learning, so the technical buyer is contacted first and an exec or intro route second.

| Segment | Accounts | Channel and why it fits |
|---|---|---|
| **Foundation-model labs** | Physical Intelligence, Skild AI, Generalist AI, Rhoda AI, Genesis AI, Dyna Robotics | DevRel first, then email. These researchers ignore sales email but read technical work. Publish a short post or notebook on labeling egocentric video for VLA fine-tuning that engages with their public research (e.g. PI's human-to-robot transfer result), share it on X and LinkedIn, then email the CTO with a labeled-sample offer against their spec. Use CoRL and RSS workshops for in-person follow-up. |
| **Funded humanoid, home and industrial robot makers** | Apptronik, Agility, 1X, Sunday, NEURA, Mind Robotics, AIM Intelligent Machines, Mobileye/Mentee | Cold email plus LinkedIn, multi-threaded. Budget exists and data collection is a stated priority. Email the CTO with a signal-specific hook, then connect on LinkedIn with the second contact two days later. Where a warm path exists, use it instead: NEURA counts Volvo Cars Tech Fund as an investor and Volvo is a Labellerr customer; AIM employs a former Oishii engineer who recommends Labellerr on its homepage; and Mind Robotics' only public contact is Rivian's CEO. All three go through intros. Book meetings ahead of robotics summits instead of cold booth visits. |
| **Customer lookalikes** | Symbotic, Carbon Robotics | Case-study-led email. Lead with the matching proof point (Coupang for warehouses, Oishii for ag robots) and ask Labellerr whether a reference call is possible. Symbotic's CTO spent nine years at Toyota, and Toyota AI is a Labellerr customer, so check for a warm path first. |
| **India** | Addverb, CynLr, Perceptyne, Ati Motors | Warm intro and in person. Indian B2B deals are relationship-driven, and proximity is Labellerr's edge. Use the Chandigarh startup ecosystem and shared investors for intros, send a LinkedIn DM, follow up on WhatsApp after first contact, and offer an on-site visit (Noida is about 250 km from Chandigarh; Hyderabad and Bengaluru are a short flight). India robotics expos are the natural event channel. |

**Sequence per account (about three weeks):**

- **Day -3 to 0:** Follow both contacts; engage with one recent post (no pitch).
- **Day 1:** Email 1 to the technical contact: signal, data problem, sample offer.
- **Day 3:** LinkedIn connection request to the second contact, referencing the same signal.
- **Day 5:** Follow-up 1 in the same thread with a useful asset (e.g. a one-page label spec for their task).
- **Day 10:** Follow-up 2 with a new angle; for labs, share the DevRel post.
- **Day 17:** Break-up note, then quarterly nurture triggered by new signals from the scoring script.

A warm intro or a pre-booked event meeting always replaces the cold sequence for Tier A accounts. Send from a warmed domain, stay under about 30 new sends a day, write in plain text, and track reply rate by segment and channel, meetings booked, and sample-to-pilot conversion. Re-run the scorer weekly.
